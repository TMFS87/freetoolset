❓ FAQ

How many coats do I need?

Two coats is standard. Use three when painting a light colour over a dark one, or when covering bare plaster.

What coverage should I enter?

Most emulsion paints cover 10–12 m² per litre per coat. The exact figure is printed on the tin — rough or porous surfaces absorb more.

❓ More Questions

How many coats do I need?

Two coats is usually enough. Going from dark to light, or painting a new wall for the first time, three coats gives more even coverage.

What coverage should I enter?

Most latex paint covers 10-12 sqm per liter per coat; the exact number is printed on the can. Rough or absorbent walls need noticeably more.

📖 Deep Dive: Paint Calculator

The calculation logic

First find the total wall area: perimeter times ceiling height = 2 times (length + width) times height. Then subtract doors and windows (standard door about 1.8 sqm, standard window about 1.5 sqm). Finally: paint needed(L) = net wall area times coats divided by coverage per liter.

An example

A 4m by 3.5m bedroom with 2.8m ceiling: perimeter 15m, total wall area 42 sqm; minus one door and one window (3.3 sqm) leaves about 38.7 sqm net. Two coats at 11 sqm/L needs about 7.03 L; buying two 5L cans is the safer choice.

Don't forget these

  • Leave a 10% margin for touch-ups, repairs, and later maintenance.
  • Primer is separate: new walls or big color changes need extra primer.
  • Ceiling is separate: area = length times width, usually with dedicated ceiling paint.
  • Dark colors use more paint: vivid red or blue may need 3-4 coats for even coverage.

    This tool is an estimate; actual usage depends on wall material, tools (rollers save paint vs spraying), and technique. Buy about 10% more than the result.
