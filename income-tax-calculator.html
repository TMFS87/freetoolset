❓ FAQ

Does moving into a higher bracket tax all my income?

No — this is the most common misunderstanding. Only the portion above each threshold is taxed at the higher rate. Earning one unit more never reduces your take-home pay.

What is the effective tax rate?

Total tax divided by total income. It is always lower than your top marginal bracket, because the lower bands are taxed at lower rates.

📖 Deep Dive: Income Tax Calculator

How progressive tax works

A progressive tax slices income into brackets, each with its own rate; only the portion falling in a bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. That is why earning one more unit can never leave you with less under a progressive system.

A concrete example

Assume rates: 0-36000 at 3%, 36000-144000 at 10%, 144000-300000 at 20%. With annual taxable income of 200000:

  • first 36000 times 3% = 1080
  • middle 108000 times 10% = 10800
  • remaining 56000 times 20% = 11200
  • total 23080, effective rate 11.5%, not the top bracket's 20%.

    • Marginal vs effective rate

    The marginal rate is the rate on your last unit of income (20% above); the effective rate is total tax divided by total income (11.5% above). Use marginal rate for raise or side-income decisions, effective rate to assess overall tax burden.

    Usage note

    This tool's default brackets are a generic example; you can edit them to match your region's actual table. The result is an estimate and excludes special deductions, social security, housing fund, and tax credits; it is not tax advice. Formal filing should follow tax authority rules or a professional.
