❓ FAQ

What is a Unix timestamp?

A Unix timestamp is the number of seconds (or milliseconds) elapsed since midnight UTC on January 1, 1970 (the "epoch"). It's a universal, timezone-free way to store time.

Seconds or milliseconds?

Many systems use seconds (10 digits, e.g. 1753216800). JavaScript and some APIs use milliseconds (13 digits). Use the dropdown to match your source.

Why do UTC and local time differ?

UTC is timezone-independent. "Your Local Time" is converted using your device's timezone setting, so it may differ from UTC by several hours.
