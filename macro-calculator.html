❓ FAQ

How many calories per gram?

Protein and carbohydrate provide 4 kcal per gram; fat provides 9 kcal per gram. That is why fat grams look small relative to its calorie share.

How much protein do I need?

General guidance is 1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight for people training regularly. Sedentary adults need less, around 0.8–1.2 g/kg.

❓ More Questions

How many calories per gram of each nutrient?

Protein and carbs are 4 kcal/g, fat is 9 kcal/g. So at the same percentage, the gram amount of fat looks much smaller.

How much protein should I eat?

Common advice for regular trainees is 1.6-2.2 g per kg of body weight; sedentary people about 0.8-1.2 g is enough. Judge based on your own health.

📖 Deep Dive: Macro Calculator

What are macronutrients

Macronutrients (often called macros) are the three energy-providing nutrients: protein, carbohydrate, and fat. Their calorie densities are: protein 4 kcal/g, carb 4 kcal/g, fat 9 kcal/g.

Common ratio plans

  • Balanced (30/40/30): good for most people's daily maintenance.
  • Low carb (35/25/40): for those with lower insulin sensitivity or who prefer high-fat diets.
  • High protein (40/35/25): for strength training or fat loss while preserving muscle.
  • High carb (25/55/20): for endurance athletes and high-volume training.

    • A conversion example

    Daily target 2000 kcal at 30/40/30: protein 600 kcal divided by 4 = 150g; carb 800 kcal divided by 4 = 200g; fat 600 kcal divided by 9 is about 67g.

    Execution tips

  • Hit protein first; it most affects satiety and muscle retention.
  • Fat should not stay below 20% of calories long term, or hormones suffer.
  • Ratios need not be exact daily; a weekly average is fine.
  • This tool is for reference; people with specific conditions should follow medical advice.
