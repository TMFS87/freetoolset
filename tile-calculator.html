📋 How to Use

  1. Enter the area length and width.
  2. Enter your tile dimensions in centimetres.
  3. Set grout gap and wastage percentage.
  4. Click Calculate to see tiles and boxes needed.

❓ FAQ

How much wastage should I allow?

Add 10% for a straight layout, 15% for diagonal or herringbone patterns, and up to 20% for small rooms with many cuts.

Why buy from the same batch?

Tile colour varies slightly between production batches. Buying all boxes at once with the same batch number avoids visible colour differences.

❓ More Questions

What waste margin is appropriate?

10% for straight layout, 15% for diagonal or herringbone, up to 20% for small rooms with many cuts. Buy a bit extra; restocking often shows color differences.

Why buy it all at once?

Tiles from different batches have subtle color differences; the same batch number (printed on the box) ensures consistent overall color.

📖 Deep Dive: Tile Calculator

The calculation method

First the tiling area: length times width. Then the effective footprint of one tile including grout: (tile length + grout) times (tile width + grout). Tiles needed = tiling area divided by single-tile footprint times (1 + waste rate).

An example

Bathroom floor 2.5m by 1.8m = 4.5 sqm, with 30cm by 30cm tiles and 2mm grout: footprint 0.302 by 0.302 is about 0.0912 sqm, theoretically 49.3 tiles; with 10% waste about 55 tiles. If 11 tiles per box, buy 5 boxes.

Why grout matters

Tiles expand and contract with temperature and need room to move; fully groutless tiling can buckle and crack when temperatures change. Standard wall tile grout 1-2mm, floor tile 2-3mm, rustic tile 3-5mm.

Practical notes

  • Note the batch number and buy from the same batch to avoid color differences.
  • Keep 2-3 whole tiles as spares for future breakage.
  • Odd-shaped areas (pipes, thresholds) need extra.
  • This tool is an estimate; rely on on-site measurement.
