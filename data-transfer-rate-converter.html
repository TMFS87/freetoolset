❓ FAQ

Why is 100 Mbps only ~12.5 MB/s?

ISPs quote bits per second (Mbps) while download managers show bytes per second (MB/s). Since 1 byte = 8 bits, divide the Mbps number by 8 to get MB/s.

Does this use 1000 or 1024?

Network speeds use decimal (1000) factors: 1 Mbps = 1,000,000 bit/s. Storage tools sometimes use 1024, but transfer rates follow the decimal convention.

What is a good home internet speed?

Roughly 25+ Mbps supports HD streaming; 100+ Mbps is comfortable for multiple 4K streams and video calls simultaneously.

📖 Deep Dive: Data Transfer Rate Converter

Bit vs byte

Network bandwidth is almost always given in bits per second (bit/s, bps), while file sizes use bytes (Byte, B), where 1 byte = 8 bits. So a 100 Mbps connection has a theoretical max download speed of about 12.5 MB/s.

Decimal conversion

Transfer rates follow the decimal system: 1 kbps = 1,000 bit/s, 1 Mbps = 1,000,000 bit/s, 1 Gbps = 10 to the 9th bit/s.

Common references

  • 4G peak: about 100 Mbps
  • Gigabit broadband: 1000 Mbps = 1 Gbps is about 125 MB/s
  • Wi-Fi 6: theoretical 9.6 Gbps

    • Notes

  • Actual speed is lower than theoretical due to line quality, congestion, and protocol overhead.
