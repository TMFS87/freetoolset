❓ FAQ

Is the bigger pack always cheaper?

No. Retail studies regularly find larger packs with a higher unit price, especially on promotion-heavy items. Always check the per-unit number.

What units should I use?

Anything, as long as all options use the same one. Comparing grams against kilograms will give a meaningless result.

📖 Deep Dive: Unit Price Calculator

Why calculate unit price

Packages vary wildly: 500g for 29.9 and 1.2kg for 68 - which is cheaper? Comparing total prices is meaningless; you must convert to a unit price under the same unit: the former is 0.0598 per g, the latter 0.0567 per g, so the larger pack wins slightly.

Common pricing traps

  • The bigger is cheaper mindset: sellers exploit this by setting a higher unit price on large packs.
  • Promo vs original: a discounted mid-size pack can beat the large pack.
  • Spec tweaks: packaging looks the same but content drops from 500g to 450g - shrinkflation.

    • How it is calculated

    Unit price = total price divided by quantity. This tool automatically finds the lowest unit price and shows how much you save versus the most expensive option.

    Practical tips

  • Store shelf labels usually show a small-print unit price, but the unit may differ.
  • Short-shelf-life items in a big pack you can't finish end up more expensive.
  • Multiply unit price by what you actually consume to get the true cost.
