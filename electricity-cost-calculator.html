📋 How to Use

  1. Enter the appliance wattage (check the label).
  2. Set daily hours of use.
  3. Enter your electricity price per kWh.
  4. Click Calculate to see daily, monthly and yearly cost.

❓ FAQ

Where do I find the wattage?

Look at the rating label on the back or underside of the appliance, or in the manual. It is shown in W (watts) or sometimes as volts × amps.

Does standby power matter?

Yes. Devices left on standby typically draw 1–5W each. Across a whole house that can add up to a noticeable share of the annual bill.

❓ More Questions

Where do I find the power rating?

Usually on the nameplate on the back or bottom of the appliance, marked in W (watts); some list only voltage and current, in which case power = voltage times current.

Does standby power matter?

Yes. A single device may use only 1-5W on standby, but a whole house of a dozen devices over a year can add up to over a hundred in cost.

📖 Deep Dive: Electricity Cost Calculator

How the electricity bill is calculated

The basic unit is the kilowatt-hour (kWh, commonly called a degree). Formula: energy(kWh) = power(W) times hours used divided by 1000; cost = energy times price.

Power reference for common appliances

  • LED bulb: about 10W
  • Laptop: about 50W
  • Desktop plus monitor: about 200W
  • Rice cooker: about 800W
  • Air conditioner (1.5 HP): about 1100W
  • Water heater: about 2000W

    • An example

    An 1100W air conditioner running 8 hours a day at 0.6 per kWh: 8.8 kWh per day, about 5.28, about 158 a month, and about 475 over a whole summer (3 months).

    Practical ways to save

  • Each 1 C higher on the AC cuts consumption about 6%-8%.
  • Use a power strip to cut standby power completely.
  • Check the energy label: tier 1 is usually 20%-30% more efficient than tier 3.
  • In time-of-use areas, run laundry and charging in off-peak hours.
