❓ FAQ

What is the density of water?

Pure water at 4°C is 1000 kg/m³ = 1 g/cm³ = 1 g/mL. This is the reference point for specific gravity.

Why is g/cm³ so common?

Because water is exactly 1 g/cm³, any material’s number immediately tells you whether it floats (below 1) or sinks (above 1).

📖 Deep Dive: Density Converter

Definition of density

Density is mass per unit volume, with the formula rho = m / V. The international unit is kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m3), but labs more often use grams per cubic centimeter (g/cm3).

Key conversions

1 g/cm3 = 1000 kg/m3 = 1 g/mL = 1 kg/L. These four expressions are numerically identical, just different unit combinations.

Common material densities (g/cm3)

  • Cork: about 0.24
  • Water: 1.00
  • Concrete: about 2.4
  • Aluminum: 2.70
  • Steel: 7.85
  • Copper: 8.96
  • Lead: 11.34
  • Gold: 19.32

    • Practical use

    Knowing density lets you convert between mass and volume. For example, a steel plate 20cm by 10cm by 5cm has a volume of 1000 cm3 and, at 7.85 g/cm3, a mass of about 7.85 kg. Shipping quotes, material purchasing, and structural self-weight calculations all rely on this relationship.
